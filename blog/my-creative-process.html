My Creative Process

February 14, 2024

People often ask how a piece starts, and the honest answer is almost always the same: a stray sentence in a notebook, or a shape I can't stop thinking about. I rarely sit down to "make art" — I sit down because something has been nagging at me for a few days and I want to see it outside my head.

Once an idea has enough weight to it, I do a round of fast, ugly sketches. These aren't meant to be shown to anyone; they're just a way of testing whether the idea survives contact with a page. If it does, I start narrowing in on composition — where the eye should land first, what's doing the emotional work, what can be simplified away.

From there, the medium usually chooses itself. Some ideas want to be a single still illustration. Others want motion, a loop that repeats just long enough to feel alive. And some ideas are better as photographs, where the "editing" happens by choosing what to leave out of the frame rather than by adding anything.

The part I don't always talk about is how much gets thrown away. For every finished piece there are usually three or four abandoned versions that just didn't hold up once the initial excitement wore off. I've stopped treating that as failure — it's closer to sorting, figuring out which idea actually wanted to exist and which one was just a warm-up for it.
